Privacy Policy

Last updated: 3 October 2026

Planora (planoraai.net) is a daily planner. This page explains, in plain words, what information Planora keeps, why, and the choices you have.

What we collect

  • Account details: your email address, the name you give (optional), and how you sign in (email and password, Google, or Apple). If you use Google or Apple, we receive your name and email address from them. We do not receive your Google or Apple password.
  • What you put into Planora: your tasks, events, goals, journal entries, study plans and the answers you give during onboarding (for example, your main goal and the time of day you work best).
  • Sign-in information: a secure sign-in session so you stay signed in on your device.

We do not use advertising, we do not track you across other websites, and we do not sell your information.

How we use it

  • To sign you in and keep your account secure.
  • To save your plans so they are the same on all your devices.
  • To make plan suggestions when you ask Planora (Ask Planora). Planora only suggests; nothing in your schedule changes until you confirm it.
  • To send emails you need, such as confirming your email address or resetting your password.

Planora does not make medical or psychological judgements about you and does not try to guess sensitive information from your journal.

Where it is stored and who helps us

  • Supabase stores your account and your Planora data, and handles sign-in. Each account's data can only be read by that account.
  • Google and Apple handle the sign-in step if you choose "Continue with Google" or "Continue with Apple".
  • OpenAI: when you use Ask Planora, the text you type and the planning details it needs (such as your tasks and free times) may be sent to OpenAI to create a suggested plan. It is not used to show you ads.
  • Google Calendar, only if you link it (see below).
  • Render runs Planora's server, which handles Ask Planora and Google Calendar requests.
  • GitHub Pages hosts the website, and Cloudflare (cdnjs) provides the icon font. Like any website, these services receive basic technical information such as your IP address when the page loads.

Documents and photos you attach (optional)

In Ask Planora you can attach a document or photo (for example a syllabus or timetable) so Planora can plan around the dates in it. The file is sent to Planora's server only to read it; photos and PDFs are read by OpenAI for this. Planora uses only the dates written in your file to make a suggested plan, which you can edit before anything is added. Planora does not keep the file after reading it.

Google Calendar (optional)

If you choose to link Google Calendar, Planora asks Google for permission to see your calendars and events. Planora doesn't change your own Google events unless you choose to (see below).

If you also turn on "Add my Planora tasks to Google Calendar", Planora asks for one more permission: to make one calendar of its own, called "Planora", in your Google account, and to add, update and remove events in that calendar only. Planora copies your timed tasks and events there (title, date and time) and keeps them up to date when you change them in Planora. It cannot touch your other calendars. You can turn this off any time; the "Planora" calendar stays in your Google account until you delete it there.

If you also choose "Change Google events from Planora", Planora asks for permission to edit events in your Google calendars. Planora only changes an event when you do it yourself in Planora (for example dragging it to a new time, or editing its name or time), and only events in calendars you own or can edit. Planora never deletes your Google events.

  • What we use: your calendar names and colours, and each event's title, date, time, location and link. We use them only to show your events in Planora's calendar, and to plan around them when you ask Planora to plan your day. If you turn on adding tasks to Google, we send the title, date and time of your Planora tasks and events to your own "Planora" calendar.
  • Where it goes: Planora's server fetches your events from Google when you open the calendar and sends them only to you. A copy of the events you've viewed is kept on your device so the calendar loads quickly. When you use Ask Planora, the times and titles of upcoming events may be sent to OpenAI along with your tasks, only to create your suggested plan.
  • Google's permission: the permission Google gives Planora is stored encrypted, linked only to your account. It is never shown to the web page or anyone else.
  • We do not sell Google data, use it for advertising, let people read it, or use it to train AI models.
  • Unlink any time: Calendar → Google → Unlink (or Profile → Google Calendar). Planora then deletes its stored permission and asks Google to cancel it. You can also remove Planora at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Deleting your Planora account removes the link too.

Planora's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

On your device

Planora keeps a copy of your plans and your sign-in session in your browser's storage so the app is fast and works offline. Logging out removes your Planora data from that device.

Your choices

  • Download your data: Profile → Privacy and data → Download my data.
  • Delete your account: Profile → Privacy and data → Delete account. This permanently deletes your account and all your Planora data (account details, tasks, events, goals, journal entries, study plans and your Google Calendar link). Nothing is kept afterwards. If you can't sign in, email sherjess11@gmail.com from the address you signed up with and we will delete your account within 30 days.
  • Change your details: you can change your name, email and password in Profile.

Children

Planora is not intended for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them.

Changes

If we change this policy, we will update the date at the top of this page.
